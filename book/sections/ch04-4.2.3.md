## 4.2.3 The constraints strategy  
〔ch04-p0052；[EPUB 原文](../epub_source/The_Anatomy_-_Corporate_Law_split_001.html)〕

<a id="ch04-p0053"></a>
The constraints strategy for protecting employees is largely embodied in dedicated regulatory structures, such as labor law, which, perhaps arbitrarily, we exclude from the purview of this book except in the context of fundamental corporate decisions, addressed in [Chapter 7](../epub_source/The_Anatomy_-_Corporate_Law_split_002.html#filepos505313) below.[66](../epub_source/The_Anatomy_-_Corporate_Law_split_010.html#filepos1116756) Other corporate law constraints for protecting non-shareholders are either toothless or narrowly targeted. For example, in some jurisdictions directors owe a duty of care to all third parties affected by the company’s operations,[67](../epub_source/The_Anatomy_-_Corporate_Law_split_010.html#filepos1116900) but in practice this duty is principally read to protect the creditors of closely held corporations.[68](../epub_source/The_Anatomy_-_Corporate_Law_split_010.html#filepos1117454) Similarly, the UK Companies Act of 2006 imposes a directorial duty to promote ‘the benefit of [company] members as a whole, and in doing so [to] have regard (amongst other matters) to … the interests of the company’s employees, … the impact of the company’s operations on the community and the environment.’[69](../epub_source/The_Anatomy_-_Corporate_Law_split_010.html#filepos1117586) But this language appears to have no constraining force, much like similar language in the typical American other-constituency statute. Finally, a possible constraint for protecting employees is the German requirement that management boards establish ‘risk management systems’ to identify and contain developments that might threaten the company’s survival.[70](../epub_source/The_Anatomy_-_Corporate_Law_split_010.html#filepos1117761) Arguably this provision injects the risk preferences of non-corporate constituencies, and especially employees, into corporate decision-making. But whether it has this effect in fact must await future litigation.[71](../epub_source/The_Anatomy_-_Corporate_Law_split_010.html#filepos1117968)
